Clinical Definition: What Is Internal Medicine?

Internal medicine is a specialized medical discipline focused exclusively on the prevention, diagnosis, and non-surgical management of adult diseases and multi-system pathology. Internal medicine physicians (internists) complete three years of intensive post-doctoral clinical residency to master complex disease interactions, metabolic health, preventative cardiology, and geriatric care.

Frequently Answered Questions

What is an Internal Medicine physician (Internist)?

An internal medicine physician (internist) is a medical doctor who specializes in the prevention, diagnosis, and non-surgical management of diseases affecting adults. Unlike family physicians who treat all age groups, internists undergo a three-year clinical residency dedicated exclusively to adult pathology, chronic illness, and geriatrics.

What chronic conditions are managed at Fairview Ogden Medical Group?

Our physicians manage Type 2 diabetes mellitus, prediabetes, hypertension, hyperlipidemia (high cholesterol), coronary artery disease, asthma, COPD, and arthritis, following evidence-based clinical protocols established by the American College of Physicians (ACP).

What is included in an annual preventative physical examination?

An annual physical includes a head-to-toe examination, vital signs assessment, personal and family health history reviews, formal medication reconciliation, age-appropriate cancer screenings, and baseline blood tests (fasting lipid profile and comprehensive metabolic panels).

Do I need to fast before my preventative appointment?

Fasting for 8 to 12 hours (water is permitted and encouraged) is typically recommended if lipid panels or blood glucose tests are scheduled. Our staff will confirm instructions when you book.
