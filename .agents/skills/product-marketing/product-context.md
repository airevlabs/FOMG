## Executive Snapshot

*[Approved paraphrase]*

Ruya is a hospital facilities and compliance software and service solution organized by building rather than user. It centralizes floor plans, life safety plans, building data, permits, project files, assets, vendors, work orders, maintenance schedules, and compliance binder records into one mobile-friendly building record. The Ruya team also handles the CAD updates that keep each building's floor plans current.

**Primary competitor (floor plans and life safety): **ATG (Advanced Technologies Group), a wholly owned JLL subsidiary that sells life safety floor plans, space utilization databases, and the AVS/CCM Plus cloud platform to health systems.

**Primary competitor (CMMS side): **FSI Software (fsiservices.com), a healthcare-focused CMMS. Ultimo, an IFS company, announced its acquisition of FSI on August 6, 2025. Accruent TMS (a Fortive company) is the other healthcare CMMS to know. Both overlap with Ruya's Work Orders, Maintenance Scheduler, and Assets solutions only.

**Outsourced services competitor: **Medxcel, an Ascension subsidiary that runs facilities management for 160+ hospitals and sells life safety assessments and life safety floor plans as a service.

## 3. Personas

*[Internal strategy] Two tiers. Buyer personas first: write to these. Then everyone else who opens the building record: write about these.*

#### User — Facility & Compliance Teams

**Role: **Facilities Directors, managers, technicians, life-safety and compliance staff.

**Cares about: **Fast access to current floor plans, permits, room data, inspections, and repairs.

**Challenge: **Documentation is scattered across binders, drives, portals, emails, and outdated floor plans.

**Value promised: **One building record, mobile CAD and BIM floor plan access with no plugins, inspections run from a phone, and Smart Maintenance Triggers.

#### Champion — System Director / VP Facilities & Construction

**Role: **System-level facility and construction leaders.

**Cares about: **A consistent system-wide operating foundation, audit readiness, and continuity through staff turnover.

**Challenge: **Fragmented tools and legacy processes across large portfolios.

**Value promised: **A single building-centric platform that standardizes records and remains current through renovations.

#### Decision Maker / Financial Buyer

**Role: **Executives and senior financial or operational analysts.

**Cares about: **Capital planning, cost reporting, space utilization, reimbursement support, and risk reduction.

**Challenge: **Incomplete space and room data disconnected from financial decisions.

**Value promised: **Room and building information tied to planning, cost centers, and portfolio visibility.

#### Technical Influencer

**Role: **Life-safety consultants, architects, general contractors, CAD/BIM specialists.

**Cares about: **Secure, simple plan updates and confidence their changes reach facility teams.

**Challenge: **Email-driven revisions and disconnected floor plan systems.

**Value promised: **Secure external access, a built-in floor plan viewer, and the tracked Update Request Process that feeds the Maintained Active Set.

#### Space & Capital Buyer — VP of Real Estate

**Role: **Health system VPs of Real Estate and space planning leaders.

**Cares about: **Room yield, which rooms generate enough patient encounters to justify their cost, where capital should go, and where there is space for third-party practices and lease agreements.

**Challenge: **Room and space data is incomplete and not connected to encounters or cost centers.

**Value promised: **Every room matched to the encounters that happened in it (Building Data), as shown in the Room to Improve case study, plus accurate floor plans and square footage to find leasable space and write lease agreements from. Ruya does not manage leases or hold lease data. Never say it does.

#### Executive — C-suite

**Role: **Health system executives. They log in to Ruya themselves.

**Cares about: **Expansion opportunities, growth opportunities, and strategic reduction and realignment across the portfolio.

**Challenge: **Portfolio decisions get made on incomplete or outdated space and building information.

**Value promised: **One current view of every building and room in the portfolio. Proof to point to: Room to Improve, 16,895 rooms measured and $24M capital moved.

## 6. Differentiation

*[Approved paraphrase]*

- Per-building organization: the building, not the user or an isolated asset, is the system of record.
- Native CAD and BIM floor plan access (PDF, DWG, and Revit) on desktop and mobile with no plugins, no downloads, and no CAD seats.
- Construction closeout records captured in Project Files so asset and vendor records are populated as projects turn over.
- Inspections run from a phone, with Smart Maintenance Triggers (Work Orders) tied to inspection and asset data.
- Secure external access: architects, life safety consultants, and vendors upload and collaborate without hospital network access, and with 0 per-seat licenses every collaborator is included.
- Hospital-specific positioning: mobile-first, compliance-aware, and built for facility teams rather than generic operations.
- Maintained Active Set: one current set of floor plans per building, with every prior revision preserved, timestamped, and attributable, kept current through the Update Request Process.
- Software and service: the Ruya team handles the floor plan CAD updates, so the hospital is not left to maintain the set alone.
## 7. Objections & Anti-Personas

*[Internal strategy]*

#### “Implementation will be heavy and IT-driven.”

Position Ruya as mobile-friendly and facilities-led, with Ruya team support for surveying, organizing, uploading, and maintaining floor plans.

#### “Our floor plans are a mess and out of date.”

Lead with the tracked Update Request Process, import and organization support from the Ruya team, and the goal of one current floor plan per building. Live line to use: "Bring your messiest building."

#### “We cannot give vendors or consultants network access.”

Use secure external access: outside parties can collaborate without needing access to the hospital network.

#### Anti-persona

Organizations seeking a broad, multi-industry CMMS for factories, offices, or general industrial operations are less aligned than hospital systems that need building-centric compliance and documentation workflows.
