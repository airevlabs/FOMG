Clinical Definition: What Is an Onsite Sleep Study (Polysomnography)?

An in-lab sleep study (polysomnography) is a comprehensive diagnostic procedure that records multi-channel biophysical changes occurring during sleep. Conducted overnight in an accredited clinical facility, it monitors electroencephalogram (EEG) brain waves, eye movements, airflow, chest expansion, and continuous arterial oxygen saturation to definitively identify obstructive sleep apnea, central apnea, and movement disorders.

Sleep Disorders Evaluated

Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA)

Upper airway obstruction causing nocturnal breathing pauses, snoring, and morning fatigue.

Central Sleep Apnea (CSA)

Neurological failure of the brain respiratory center to signal diaphragm breathing during sleep.

Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS)

Involuntary nocturnal limb twitches and micro-arousals disrupting slow-wave deep sleep.

Narcolepsy & Hypersomnia

Disrupted REM architecture and severe daytime sleep attacks requiring multi-latency sleep testing.

Frequently Answered Questions

What is an in-lab polysomnography (PSG) sleep study?

A polysomnogram (PSG) is the gold-standard overnight diagnostic sleep study established by the American Academy of Sleep Medicine. Conducted in a private suite, it non-invasively monitors brain waves (EEG), ocular movement, airflow, heart rate, chest expansion, and continuous blood oxygen levels.

How does an in-lab study compare to an at-home sleep test (HST)?

While at-home tests capture limited respiratory flow, only an accredited in-lab study records comprehensive 16-channel EEG brain wave activity to evaluate sleep architecture, limb movements, central apneas, and allow real-time CPAP pressure titration.

Is an overnight sleep study covered by insurance and Medicare?

Yes. Diagnostic polysomnography and CPAP titration are covered benefits under Medicare, Illinois Medicaid, and private commercial insurance when sleep apnea or sleep disorder symptoms are documented.
